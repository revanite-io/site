Very few people are aware of the tenuous financial ties that exist between software producers, product manufacturers, nation states, and the North Pole.

Truthfully, *licensing* is the largest expense to Santa’s Workshop. It’s been a curse since the height of the industrial revolution, when Santa almost started an international conflict by destabilizing the toy industry.

Now, things are different from the way they used to be. Every time a kid somewhere in the world asks for a brand-name toy, Santa’s elves scramble to secure a licensing agreement to allow magical production of the items. And sometimes a deal can’t be reached, which means that the elves have to do their most dreaded task… negotiate with suppliers.

For the first six months every year, the elf Sandabüts goes through the annual slog of conversations, paperwork, and planning to make sure that the workshop has everything it needs for both the magical and physical production lines. Then for another five months he works to coordinate team leads across the workshop to keep things moving toward Christmas day.

But in the last week of November in the year of 2026, Santa pulled his Christmas Operations Officer into an impromptu conversation on the balcony overlooking Workshop #1.

“Sandy, it seems you overlooked something.”

Sandabüts racked his brain to imagine what Santa could be referencing. Did an elf hide a failure on one of the production lines? Did the reindeer fail to collect a shipment of letters from children? Was there a problem at one of their suppliers?

“For decades now, we’ve relied on airspace approvals for our deliveries on Christmas day,” Santa began. “If we don’t remain in good graces with the big governments, we can’t fulfill our mission.”

Sandabüts nodded, but still didn’t understand what Santa was going on about. It was clear on his face, and Santa noticed.

“I’m talking about regulations, little elf.”

Sandy didn’t like when Santa used diminutives when speaking to him — the person who singlehandedly ensured that Christmas happened every year.

“Santa, we’ve followed everything—”

“There is something new. And I’ve just been put on notice. It seems the European Union is quite eager to show that they’re serious about their new Cyber Resilience Act.”

“But that doesn’t apply yet!” Sandabüts protested. “We have until 2027, and even then… we’re not sure it even applies to us!”

Then Santa said the most terrifying words a COO can ever be blind-sided by.

“I’ve privately consulted with a subject matter expert.” Santa’s face tightened, a sign that Sandabüts knew too well. The discussion was over, and whatever Santa says next is what will happen.

“Reporting requirements began in September of this year. That means any manufacturer of products with digital elements will need to report exploited vulnerabilities through ENISA’s Single Reporting Platform within 24 hours.”

“Yes, and a full notification within 72 hours. I’m aware. But that doesn’t apply to us! We just *distribute* the toys with digital elements!”

Santa’s eyes narrowed, revealing impatience. “That’s true for any toys that are fully complete when we buy them. But anything we assemble, or any toys we are licensed to magically produce… the expert thinks we’re on the hook for those.”

Sandabüts pressed his luck, trying to avoid more work in the final weeks before Christmas.

“This is just about known exploited vulnerabilities. We can do a quick search to see if anything comes up and we’ll just fix it before the toys go out. It’s probably nothing, anyhow.”

Santa nodded, “Yes, that’s a start. But perhaps it’s time I introduce you to our expert. They advise that we will need to issue notices if something is found later… Even after the toys are in children's hands.”

“But that’s… that’s impossible to keep track of!”

“That’s why you’re going to build a comprehensive automated governance program.”

Sandabüts couldn’t restrain his smirk. Santa sounded funny when he used industry jargon and buzz words. That smirk backfired.

“It is possible, and you’ll have it done before Christmas. I want a database of reports showing that you audited every toy we built before it gets on my sleigh, and I want to know that we have a comprehensive system to automate continuous evaluations and alerts for the next ten years.”

“That’s… that’s *beyond* unreasonable!”

“Based on what I’ve been reading, it seems we can hit our deadlines if we adopt the *Gemara* ecosystem. It looks complicated, though. So I’ve hired the consultant to come in person.”

Sandabüts was flabbergasted. They hadn’t had outsiders at the North Pole since the installation of the missile defense systems in the 1980s.

“They’ll be here for 24 days. Make sure to use their time wisely… I don’t want to hear from a regulator just because you couldn’t get some paper pushing tools in place.”

With that final derisive comment, Santa waved his hand through the air. In a burst, he turned into a stream of sparkles that wisped across the workshop and out into the icy night.
